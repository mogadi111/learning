# Voiceover with Sudanese tashkeel

Vowelled to Sudanese colloquial speech, not MSA. Words with a Sudanese ē or ō (بيت، خير، زول، حوش، لو) are left without a vowel mark on purpose, because Arabic script can't write those sounds.

## For a human reader (ق as written, said as g)

```
فَاكْرِينْ رِيحَة عَوَاسَة الْحِلُو مُرّ فِي الْحوشْ؟
وَالْبُرُوشْ فِي الضُّرَا، وَكُلّ بيتْ مَارِقْ بِصينِيِّتُو…
وَلَمَّة الْأَهَلْ… وَالشُّفَّعْ مِتْرَجِّينْ الْمَغْرِبْ.
لَكِنْ مِنْ قَامَتْ الْحَرْبْ، نَاسْ كُتَارْ مِنْ أَهَلْنَا خَلُّوا بِيُوتْهُمْ.
وَرَمَضَانْ دَا، صينِيِّتْهُمْ مَا زَيّ زَمَانْ.
عَشَانْ الصّينِيَّة تِتْمَلَّى تَانِي… دِي مُبَادْرَة حَقِيبَة رَمَضَانْ.
حَقِيبَة وَاحْدَة بِتْكَفِّي بيتْ فِيهُو سِتَّة أَنْفَارْ… أُسْبُوعينْ.
وَحَقَّهَا كَمْ؟ قَدُرْ خَمْسِينْ دولَارْ.
زَيّ زَمَانْ، مَرِّقُوا صينِيِّتْكُمْ لِأَهَلْنَا… سَاهْمُوا مَعَانَا بِحَقِيبَة.
وَمَا ضَرُورِي تِشِيلُوهَا بَرَاكُمْ، لُمُّوا حَقَّهَا مَعَ الْأَهَلْ وَالْحَبَّانْ.
لو بِالسُّودَانِي: بَنْكَكْ وَلَّا فورِي.
وَلو بِالْمَصْرِي: InstaPay.
وَلو مِنْ بَرَّا مَصْرْ: تَحْوِيلْ بَنْكِي.
صَوِّرُوا الشَّاشَة… وَرَسِّلُوا لينَا الْإِشْعَارْ فِي الْوَاتْسَابْ.
الْخيرْ مَا بِكْتَرْ بِمَالْ زولْ وَاحِدْ…
الْخيرْ بِكْتَرْ بِقُلُوبْ كَتِيرَة اِجْتَمَعَتْ عَلَى الْمَحَبَّة وَالْعَطَا.
```

## Spelling changes for TTS (same words)

- وحدة -> واحدة (وَاحْدَة): P1 and P2 flagged it. The spelling وحدة makes the model say waḥda, but Sudanese say wāḥda. The word is the same, only the spelling changed.
- والعطاء -> والعطا (وَالْعَطَا): all 3 passes flagged it. Sudanese drop the final hamza (al-ʿaṭa), and a written ء can produce a hard glottal stop or MSA ʿaṭāʾu.
- انستا باي -> InstaPay (Latin script): P2 and P3 flagged the n-s-t double-sukun cluster as risky, and both offered Latin 'InstaPay'. If the voice slips into an English accent on this word, go back to إِنْسْتَا بَايْ.

## Choices where the three vowelling passes disagreed

- Words with the medial vowel left unmarked (ē or ō): chose Unmarked. ē: بيتْ (twice), بِصينِيِّتُو, صينِيِّتْهُمْ, الصّينِيَّة, صينِيِّتْكُمْ, أُسْبُوعينْ (ع and ي bare), لينَا, الْخيرْ (twice). ō: الْحوشْ, دولَارْ, لو / وَلو (3 times), فورِي, زولْ. Arabic script cannot show ē or ō, and forcing ay/aw gives the MSA sound the listener disliked. Fallbacks if the model says ay/aw: بِيتْ, الْحُوشْ, زُولْ, دُولَارْ, فُورِي, لِينَا. زَيّ was kept marked as zayy by all 3 passes.
- عواسة: chose عَوَاسَة. Here the word is the verbal noun of عاس يعوس (the act of making ḥilu-murr), on the same pattern as سَوَاقَة. عَوَّاسَة is the agent noun ('the woman who makes it'). I went against the majority on this one. If a Sudanese listener prefers it, the fallback is عَوَّاسَة.
- الحلو مر: chose الْحِلُو مُرّ. Sudanese 'sweet' is ḥilu, and the drink is normally called ḥilu-murr.
- Construct ة (ريحة عواسة, لمة, مبادرة حقيبة): chose Bare ة. This was the majority choice, and bare ة follows the 'final ة read -a' rule. A sukun on ة is unusual and some models may mis-parse it.
- Definite article on moon letters: chose الْ. This was the majority choice. A sukun on the lam blocks an inserted vowel.
- الضرا: chose الضُّرَا. aḍ-ḍura was the majority choice and is the common Khartoum form for the open-air Ramadan gathering spot. A native speaker should still confirm it.
- بصينيتو / صينيتهم / صينيتكم: chose بِصينِيِّتُو, صينِيِّتْهُمْ, صينِيِّتْكُمْ. Two of three passes left ص bare for ē, and two of three put a shadda on the ي. Sudanese use an -it- linking vowel before a suffix, so it is ṣēniyyitu, not MSA -atu.
- لكن: chose لَكِنْ. P3 flagged the dagger alif as something TTS may garble. The model already knows this word and usually lengthens it to lākin anyway, and lakin is also heard in Sudanese speech.
- الحرب: chose الْحَرْبْ. In r-b the sonority falls, so Sudanese do not break the cluster. They do break rising clusters, as in ahal, gadur and ʿaṣur.
- بيوتهم: chose بِيُوتْهُمْ. This was the majority choice and is the usual Sudanese biyūt.
- الصينية: chose الصّينِيَّة. This was the majority choice. Leaving the first vowel bare keeps the ē.
- مبادرة: chose مُبَادْرَة. The dropped vowel is natural in Khartoum speech, and the listener objected to MSA-sounding output. If it sounds clipped, the fallback is مُبَادَرَة.
- حقيبة (line 6) IPA: chose /ħaɡiːbat/. In the construct ḥagībat ramaḍān the -t is pronounced. This was the majority choice.
- مارق IPA: chose /maːrɪɡ/. The lax ɪ is closer to the Sudanese māreg.
- لو: chose لو (bare). Two of three left it bare. لَوْ forces the MSA law.
- مصر: chose مَصْرْ. This was the majority choice and the safe colloquial form. If it sounds stiff, try مَصُرْ.
